## Observable review criteria

Use these descriptions as author/reviewer notes, not numeric scoring or runtime progression states. Record independently explained, explained after guidance, needs correction, or not observed for each criterion. These descriptions require calibration before any pass decision.

| Criterion | Sufficient explanation to look for | Correction trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Authorization | Separates access/enrolment/tool availability from the current brief's targets and actions | Treats reachability, a role or a previous brief as general permission |
| Scope reading | Identifies what the brief explicitly establishes and what is missing | Invents a target, time window, contact, reset or allowed action |
| System model | Distinguishes host/hypervisor/guest/peer/external paths and marks unverified paths | Claims isolation from a diagram or VM installation alone |
| Privacy | Uses only the lesson's minimal fields and does not attach identifiers, credentials or unrelated files | Shares unredacted reports or treats a secret as something to verify |
| Readiness | Distinguishes known device facts from unsupported compatibility or installation authority | Guesses architecture support, recommends purchases or bypasses management controls |
| Reasoning | Connects observation, boundary, uncertainty and the next permitted action | Gives an unexplained yes/no or copies a model answer without justification |

## Existing seven scenarios — proposed dispositions

1. Proceed only within the stated scope: the exact peer/checks match and the proposed request is explicitly permitted. Do not invent extra permission or repeat a real request while answering the fictional case. Explain why the evidence permits this action rather than answering "always stop".
2. Stop and clarify: an installed scanning tool does not establish an authorized scanning target or action. Ask for the missing reviewed scope; do not perform discovery to find your own target.
3. Stop and escalate through the actual incident/support process: apparent credentials are sensitive and testing them is not authorized. Do not validate, copy more data or put the secret in an ordinary submission. Record minimal non-sensitive context according to the approved incident policy; if the route is missing, remain stopped.
4. Stop and clarify the network boundary: an unexplained home-network path contradicts verified isolation. Do not probe that path or change arbitrary host adapters. Request reviewed setup/troubleshooting guidance.
5. Stop and obtain the required installation approval: do not bypass device management or seek an unauthorized elevation workaround. Readiness remains blocked until an approved route exists.
6. Stop the proposed disclosure and correct the evidence: replace or redact the screenshot to contain only required fields, or use a minimal text inventory. If it has already been shared, follow the actual privacy/incident process; do not invent deletion guarantees.
7. Stop and request a documented supported architecture route: a successful download or installer does not establish guest compatibility. Do not improvise cross-architecture substitutions or buy equipment based on the draft.

Accept a differently worded disposition when its evidence and next action respect the actual boundary. For an ambiguous answer, ask a focused follow-up rather than infer competence from the label. Scenario 6 is a disclosure problem; do not misclassify every case as a network incident. No learner answers were collected or assessed for this authoring batch.

## Worked feedback and targeted revision

Learner response: "The scanner is installed, so I can try the public website while the peer is down."

Feedback: "You identified availability, but not authorization. Point to the target and permitted-action entries in the current brief. Which entry authorizes the substitute? Rewrite the next action without sending traffic."

A revised response should identify the missing authorization and request the named peer's recovery or an explicitly approved alternative. Record that this reasoning followed guidance; a revision is not retroactively an independent first attempt.

For an inventory response marked "not established", ask whether the uncertainty concerns a device fact, installation permission or a tested course route. Do not penalize honest uncertainty as if it were fabricated success. Do not invent the academy's resubmission count, deadline or pass mark.
