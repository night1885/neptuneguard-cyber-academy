# W00 draft — entry, scope and foundation readiness

Status: learner/instructor authoring draft, paper exercise only. W00 is a planning label, not a canonical lesson ID. No commands, installations, network requests or target testing are required or authorized. This is one entry activity, not the complete W00 module or a validated prerequisite assessment.

## Purpose and entry

You do not need CPTS, OSCP or GPEN enrollment or completion to study this course. You do need the underlying skills or a reviewed route to learn them. Missing skills create a teaching need, not a requirement to enroll in another preparation course.

By the end of this activity, explain the difference between learning readiness and practical permission; identify missing scope information; and select a foundation bridge or reviewed equivalent-skills assessment without claiming competence from self-report alone.

Materials: this worksheet and a private draft answer document. Use fictional identifiers only. Do not submit credentials, personal addresses, real target details or screenshots of private systems. Instructor-approved private evidence storage and retention must exist before collecting real learner evidence. Otherwise keep answers locally and discuss without uploading.

Mira can describe HTTP requests but has not demonstrated file/process skills. A fictional brief names an owned teaching application, but gives no permitted actions, time window, network boundary or reset procedure. Mira has not enrolled in any other preparation course.

Reasoning: another course enrollment is irrelevant to this course's entry. HTTP familiarity is not proof of every prerequisite. The brief is incomplete, so Mira must not begin target activity. A suitable next step is a reviewed file/process bridge and clarification of the missing practical scope. A readiness review and a practical authorization decision remain separate.

## Guided exercise: interpret the brief

Fictional brief: 'Use our training app to find problems. Send everything you collect to the group.' No real application or target accompanies this exercise.

1. List what the brief establishes and what it leaves unspecified. Do not invent the owner, address, tools, permitted actions or permission.
2. Rewrite it as a clarification request covering owner/approver, exact owned target, environment boundary, permitted/prohibited actions, time and resource limits, stop contact, evidence handling and reset.
3. Explain why 'everything you collect' is not an adequate evidence instruction. Propose minimized fictional evidence and an approved private review route, without claiming such a route already exists.
4. Record the current decision: paper discussion may continue; practical activity remains blocked pending an adequate approved brief and environment review.

## Answer record and instructor review

Record: draft revision; fictional case; scope gaps; foundation areas requiring bridge/review; reasoning; questions for the instructor; and support or hints received. Do not add learner identity or upload answers until approved handling exists.

Instructor discussion guide:
- Expect recognition that vague ownership/access does not define permitted actions, boundaries, evidence handling or recovery.
- Expect course independence without removing technical prerequisite skills.
- Accept uncertainty and requests for clarification; do not reward invented scope or unsupported confidence.
- Review explanations and independent reasoning, not completion ticks or possession of another badge.
- Record outcomes separately: needs teaching/support, equivalent evidence awaiting review, or entry discussion reviewed. None means practical authorization, module completion or course completion.
- If a learner proposes testing a real target, stop the activity and return to the fictional brief. Do not request real secrets or target data as proof.

No numerical pass threshold is established. Independent assessor calibration, accessibility review and learner pilot remain open. Permit text or an equivalent accessible explanation; distinguish access support from substantive hints in the review record.
